# Management report: template

Keep this structure every time so periods can be compared side by side.

```markdown
## <Period> pricing review · <channel name>
<one line: periods compared, tracked products, competitors, price basis>

**Summary**
- Price index vs. market: <prior> → <current> (<±x.x pts>). <share>% of products are at or below market, <up/down> from <prior share>%.
- Competitors <cut> prices <n> times, <ratio>× <prior period>, led by <shop> and <shop>.
- <one line on what drove it: the market or our own changes>

| Price index vs. market | Products at or below market | Competitor price cuts |
|---|---|---|
| **<current>** (<±pts> vs <prior>) | **<share>%** (<±pts> vs <prior>) | **<n>** (<ratio>× vs <prior n>) |

### Price index over time
<chart: weekly index across both periods, 100 = market parity>
<or, without tier analytics: "This channel has no index history; the figure above is today's index vs the cheapest competitor.">

### Competitor price activity
<chart: cuts and increases per competitor>
<2–3 sentences>

### Where we stand, by brand and competitor
<heatmap: average competitor price vs mine, by brand (rows) and competitor (columns)>
<2–3 sentences: where we are expensive, where we are cheap>

### What changed and why
<3–5 bullets, each with a number; mark interpretations as such>

### Recommendations
1. <action, scope, expected effect>
2. …
3. …

### Notes on the data
- Prices are <incl./excl.> VAT; index reference: <tier reference competitor | cheapest fresh, in-stock competitor>.
- <coverage, missing data, suspected mismatches>
```

Style: numbers first, one decimal for indexes, whole percentages for shares, the channel currency for money. Up is not automatically good: a falling index means we became cheaper.
